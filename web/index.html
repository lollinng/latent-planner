How it works

  1. Learn. From 2,000 random trajectories (98k frames), an encoder maps each 64×64 frame to a 32-number embedding, and a predictor learns to guess the next embedding from the current one and the action. The loss is the LeWM / LeJEPA recipe: next-embedding error plus SIGReg, a term that keeps embeddings spread like a Gaussian so they can't collapse. No pixels are ever reconstructed.
  2. Imagine. To plan, the browser samples hundreds of action sequences and runs the predictor forward 20 steps for each, entirely in embedding space.
  3. Choose. The cross-entropy method keeps the sequences whose imagined embeddings get closest to the goal image's embedding, refines them for a few rounds, executes the first 4 steps, then plans again from the new frame.

The dotted path is decoded with a linear read-out fitted after training, only to draw it; the planner never sees positions. Turn on the cost map to see what the planner optimises: distance in embedding space is a sharp basin around the goal and flat elsewhere, not a map of the rooms. Routes through the door come from imagining far enough ahead to find the basin.

Environment, model and planner are written from scratch for this demo. Objective: LeWM (Maes et al.) and LeJEPA's SIGReg (Balestriero & LeCun, 2025). Code and write-up.
