# Planner-side fixes for sampling-based MPC with imperfect learned world models on pushing, plus practitioner notes

Reading guide (ranked by relevance to our failure mode: long pushes that need a walk-around, and more search not helping with the learned model). Sections below follow this order.
1. Aim the planner at a near, on-manifold target instead of the final goal (anchored / retrieved targets, learned subgoals, hierarchy). Best-supported fix for long PushT pushes: flat LeWM 7.0% -> 69.5% at PushT offset 140 (Anchored Planning); DINO-WM 17% -> 61% at d=75 (HWM); 4.7% -> 64.7% at offset 150 (SAGE). All 2026 preprints.
2. Robustness to model error: restrict search to data-supported actions, check action clipping, terminal-only cost, ensembles, replan cadence. Evidence that extra search lowers model cost while losing real successes on PushT.
3. Replace raw latent L2 with a learned temporal-distance / value cost. Large gains on navigation (TwoRoom 26% -> 98%), weaker or indirect evidence on pushing.
4. What DINO-WM / LeWM / PLDM / V-JEPA 2-AC / TD-MPC2 actually use; gradient vs CEM (gradient never beats CEM on PushT in the sources found).
5. CEM/MPPI internals (iCEM etc.): mostly sample-efficiency gains, not robustness to model error.
6. Practitioner notes from GitHub issues (eval noise of +-5-6 pp at 50 episodes, config mismatches, train-split evaluation, context-length bug).

Caveat for the whole file: most PushT-specific planner results are 2026 arXiv preprints (many unreviewed, several single-author). Numbers are as reported by the authors; I read abstracts or HTML extracts, not always the full tables.

## Hierarchical / subgoal planning for long pushes in latent space

### Takeaway
For long PushT pushes, every 2026 source that changes *what the planner aims at* (a near target that lies on a recorded route) reports large gains over flat CEM to the final goal, while extra final-goal search adds almost nothing; the gains come from targets drawn from data (retrieved segments, encoded training macro-actions, learned subgoal generators), and unconstrained high-level search re-introduces model exploitation.

### Cited Findings
- **Anchored Planning (AP), training-free, frozen LeWM.** Retrieves a recorded segment from the world model's own offline data that leads from the current observation toward the goal, then aims the frozen planner at the frame 5 steps after that segment's start (one action block ahead). Retrieval is nearest-neighbour on frozen LeWM embeddings with key (start, start+h, displacement) matched against (current, goal, goal - current), span h(t) = max{5, H - t} — [Liu et al. 2026, arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- AP results at the original long offsets (PushT 140 actions, Cube 150, Reacher 150, TwoRoom 100), success %: PushT — flat LeWM 7.0, single-block CEM to final goal 2.3, AP-CEM 69.5, ranking retrieved blocks against final goal 25.8, AP-rank 66.4. Cube 15.6 / 7.0 / 46.9 / 62.5 / 73.4. Mean over 4 tasks: LeWM 26.6 vs AP-rank 83.8 — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- AP planner settings: single-block CEM, 300 candidates, 30 elites, 30 iterations over one 5-action block; AP-rank scores 8 retrieved action blocks; replan after at most 5 actions. LeWM baseline plans 5 blocks x 5 actions — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- Directly on our failure mode: "all 89 AP-CEM PushT successes take a detour and 100 of 125 final-goal CEM failures stall" (Table 7). A proposition shows that scoring by final-goal distance can make "do nothing" the global optimum when the route first moves away from the goal, *even with exact dynamics and globally optimal short-horizon search* — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- More search does not fix the final-goal objective: PushT CEM-to-final-goal 2.3% at 30 iterations vs 1.6% at 1 iteration; AP-CEM 8.6% at 2 iterations and 69.5% at 30 — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- AP ablations (mean over tasks): target offset l = 1/3/5/10 steps past the matched record start (default 5) gives AP-CEM 43.0 / 69.9 / 59.8 / 54.1; memory 10% / 25% / 50% / 100% of data gives AP-rank 77.5 / 80.1 / 82.0 / 83.8; replacing the shrinking span with a fixed span H collapses AP-rank to 9.6 — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- **HWM (Meta FAIR et al.).** Two world models in a shared latent space; high-level model conditioned on learned latent macro-actions; high-level CEM minimizes L1 to goal and passes its first intermediate latent as the low-level subgoal; both replan closed-loop — [Zhang, ..., LeCun, Ballas 2026, arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- HWM PushT success at start-goal distance d = 25 / 50 / 75 steps: DINO-WM flat 84 / 55 / 17%, DINO-WM hierarchical 89 / 78 / 61%; HIQL 55 / 30 / 20%; GCIQL 40 / 25 / 7.5%; HILP 25 / 13 / 0% — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- HWM PushT planner settings: d=25 high k=5, budget B=10, H=2, 900 samples; low h=5, 300 samples. d=50 high B=20, H=4, 1500 samples, EMA 0.9; low h=5, 900 samples, EMA 0.8. d=75 high B=25, H=5, 1200 samples, EMA 0.9; low h=5, 1200 samples, EMA 0.8. PushT high-level segments are 25-70 steps with N=5 waypoints — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- HWM: hierarchy reaches higher success with up to 3x less compute per planning step on PushT; a larger flat model did not help (DINO-WM flat 98M params: 35% at d=50 vs 55% at 44M; hierarchical 94M: 78%); macro-actions beat concatenated primitive actions as the high-level action (Maze 95% vs 52%, 83% vs 37%) — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- **Hi-LeWM ("Mind the Gap").** Frozen low-level LeWM + high-level search over latent subgoals on PushT and Cube: +11.3 pp at medium and +14.7 pp at the longest PushT horizon over flat LeWM. With true future latents as subgoals the frozen low-level controller tracks well, so subgoal generation is the bottleneck. "Unconstrained search can select latent macro-actions that appear favorable under the learned model" but are poor control targets; restricting high-level search to macro-actions encoded from training trajectories recovered useful regimes — [Caselli et al. 2026, arXiv 2607.12547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12547)
- A search snippet of the Hi-LeWM paper reports a flat LeWM PushT baseline of about 18% at budget 150 with horizon 5 and a CEM budget reduced from 300x30 (not verified in the full text) — [arXiv 2607.12547 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12547)
- **SAGE.** Replaces random CEM-style proposal initialization with a goal-conditioned generator that predicts the next latent subgoal and then generates candidate action sequences conditioned on it; the frozen world model scores and refines them. PushT at target offset 150: 4.7% -> 64.7%; OGBench Cube offset 150: 20.7% -> 67.3%; LIBERO full episodes 0% (vanilla LeWM planner) -> 48.7% / 58% — [Cheng, Zhang, Wang 2026, arXiv 2607.17973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17973)
- **FF-JEPA** adds an action-free latent planner that predicts subgoal latents every H steps on top of a frozen LeWM; only "preliminary PushT results" claimed to overcome long-horizon collapse, no numbers seen — [arXiv 2606.09311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09311)
- **LeFlow**: a rectified-flow model generates a latent path from current to goal embedding, an inverse-dynamics decoder turns it into action chunks, the frozen world model verifies candidates; abstract claims consistent success gains and an order-of-magnitude lower planning time, no numbers in the abstract — [Huang et al. 2026, arXiv 2608.24855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24855). A search snippet says its matched CEM baseline (DINO-WM official config 300 samples, 30 elites, 30 iterations) reached 84% on 50 PushT cases — [arXiv 2608.24855 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24855)
- **Planning-horizon limit, independent of the model.** On Meta-World with frozen-backbone predictors trained on 5-step rollouts, a world model ranks actions reliably only for targets about 5-10 control steps ahead, while goals lie 16-53 steps away; *with the real simulator as predictor*, success drops from 92% to 41% as the target moves from 5 to 20 steps beyond a 5-step rollout. Distant goals: pure imagination 23%, MPC 30%, imagining as far as the goal 47%, nearby expert subgoals 76%. An 81x larger predictor did not extend the range — [Alrasheed, Azam, Akhtar 2026, arXiv 2609.39235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39235)
- V-JEPA 2-AC, single goal image, flat: 0% pick-and-place (cup, box) on Franka; with HWM hierarchy 70% / 60%; flat with manual subgoals 80% / 80% — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208). The original V-JEPA 2-AC pick-and-place already used three hand-given subgoal images (4, 10, 4 steps) — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)

### Inferences
- Our oracle result (true simulator + true-state cost: 57%) matches AP's proposition and the Planning-Limits simulator result: a final-goal distance cost is a bad objective for walk-around pushes even with perfect dynamics. So part of the long-push failure is objective/target, not only model error.
- Our graph-of-embeddings subgoals did not help push. AP differs in ways that plausibly matter: (a) the key includes the goal-minus-current displacement, so it retrieves a segment heading the right way rather than a shortest graph path; (b) the target is only one block (5 steps, best l = 3) past the matched start, so it is always within the model's reliable range; (c) the span shrinks with remaining budget (fixed span collapses to 9.6%); (d) AP-rank executes recorded action blocks, which are on-distribution and cannot be exploited. Worth re-trying subgoals with these four properties before concluding subgoals do not work for push.
- "Execute retrieved data actions, re-ranked by the model" (AP-rank 66.4% PushT) is the cheapest experiment for us: no training, uses our 32-d embeddings, and directly tests whether exploitation of CEM-sampled actions is the problem.
- Hierarchy with a learned high-level model (HWM) is a bigger lift (second model, macro-action encoder) and gives similar long-range gains to AP; try AP first.

### Gaps
- AP's per-offset PushT sweep (Figure 3 / Table 12) was not in the extracted text, so I have no number at offset ~25-50 comparable to our tuning tasks.
- No absolute success numbers for Hi-LeWM, FF-JEPA or LeFlow on PushT beyond the deltas/snippets above.
- No source tested graph shortest-path subgoals over training embeddings on PushT and reported failure, so I cannot confirm why ours failed.

## Robustness to model error at plan time (exploitation, cost shaping, replanning, uncertainty)

### Takeaway
There is direct PushT evidence that more optimization against a learned latent model lowers predicted cost while turning real successes into failures, and that the learned predictor's candidate ranking on PushT is weak (physical Spearman 0.19); the fixes with reported effects are keeping candidates on the data manifold (retrieved actions, encoded training macro-actions, action-norm bounds), ensemble-uncertainty penalties (PLDM), and checking that predicted and executed actions are clipped identically; LeWM, DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC all use terminal-only costs.

### Cited Findings
- **More planning hurts on PushT.** In a matched experiment (first ten commands replayed, then the next solve given 4, 8 or 12 action updates), success went 9 -> 8 -> 7; extra updates rescued no failing task, and in one scene cluster they lowered the world-model cost of the chosen trajectory while previously successful tasks failed. Planner comparison on 16 held-out PushT scenes: tuned Adam 7/16 (218 s), adapted iCEM 2/16 (49 s), released LePlanner 16/16 (5.7 s), proposed 1,505-parameter learned updater 8/16 (59 s) — [Chen 2026, arXiv 2610.37891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37891) (single-author preprint)
- **Ranking quality of a LeWM-class predictor on PushT.** Over 300 shared candidates per start, Spearman between predicted latent cost and physical outcome is 0.193 for LeWM on PushT; encoding the realized endpoints instead of predicting them raises it to 0.631 (Reacher 0.464 -> 0.975; Cube's encoded-goal cost stays uninformative). Low latent MSE does not mean better action selection: in 6 of 16 model-task cells, fixing only action-relative responses beats fixing the dominant "center" error for elite quality — [Min et al. 2026, arXiv 2610.05550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05550)
- **Practitioner exploitation probe (LeWM, TwoRoom/PushT).** A retrained predictor with 11x lower latent error planned 36% vs 85%; the chosen CEM plans had predicted cost 27x below realized. Root cause was a training bug (only the last of three context positions supervised; the planner starts from one frame: error 1.18 at 1-frame context vs 0.006 at 3). Later control: comparing predictions for *unclipped* candidate actions with *clipped* executions inflated apparent optimism (ratio fell from about 18.1 to 1.45 after clipping) — [le-wm issue #107](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/107); [sahnas summary](https://github.com/sahnas/stigmergic-module-routing/blob/main/docs/summary_two_pages.md)
- Same practitioner: choosing CEM's final output as elite mean vs best final candidate changed success by only -1 / 0 / +1 pp (3,600 episodes); a ridge bilinear predictor planned better than the neural one on TwoRoom (96-100% vs 81-89%) but collapsed on PushT (35-40% vs 85-94%) — [sahnas summary](https://github.com/sahnas/stigmergic-module-routing/blob/main/docs/summary_two_pages.md)
- **Constraining search to data support.** Hi-LeWM: unconstrained high-level search picks macro-actions that look good to the model but are poor targets; restricting to macro-actions encoded from training trajectories fixes it — [arXiv 2607.12547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12547). V-JEPA 2-AC constrains each sampled action to an L1 ball of radius 0.075 because "larger actions are largely out-of-distribution for the models" — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1). A 2026 deployment-time planning paper samples from a data-informed proposal because implausible actions push the world model off-distribution and yield exploitable errors (search snippet) — [Crosby et al. 2026, arXiv 2610.19579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19579)
- **Ensemble uncertainty penalty (PLDM).** Goal cost averaged over K predictors and summed over all steps t = 0..H; uncertainty cost = sum of per-dimension ensemble variance weighted by gamma^t; total = goal + beta * uncertainty; ensemble of 5 MLP predictors for Ant-U-Maze; the term penalizes transitions that "deviate from the training distribution" — [Sobal et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14819v4). The beta value and the Appendix E ablation were not in the extracted text.
- TD-MPC2 as packaged in stable-worldmodel adds an uncertainty penalty on the terminal Q-value with a conservatism coefficient c — [stable-worldmodel baselines](https://galilai-group.github.io/stable-worldmodel/baselines/)
- Offline-RL penalties (MOReL, MOPO) and COPlanner use model uncertainty as a rollout penalty; general mechanism, no pushing-planner numbers found — [search result listing incl. arXiv 2310.07220](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07220v2)
- **Cost shaping.** LeWM cost is terminal-only, ||z_H - z_g||^2 — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312). DINO-WM: terminal-only ||z_T - z_g||^2 — [arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2). V-JEPA 2-AC: terminal-only L1 — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1). PLDM: sum over all steps, no discount — [arXiv 2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14819v4). HWM high level: L1 to goal — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- Latent Euclidean distance as cost on reproduced LeWM TwoRoom: correlation with true distance r = 0.426, saturating by about 80 arena units and *decreasing* beyond 120 (moving away can lower cost) — [Singh 2026, arXiv 2608.12959](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12959)
- **Replan cadence.** PLDM, TwoRoom, normalized success vs replanning every 1 / 4 / 16 / 32 steps: 1.00 / 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.62 (time per episode 16.0 / 4.8 / 2.6 / 2.2 s) — [arXiv 2502.14819 App. F](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14819v4). LeWM App. D executes the whole 5-block (25-step) plan before replanning (Sec. 3.2 says "first K", K unspecified) — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312). V-JEPA 2-AC executes only the first action — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1). DINO-WM: MPC with replanning 0.90 vs open-loop CEM 0.86 on PushT — [arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- **Representation-side fixes that change the cost landscape** (model-side, listed for completeness): inverse-dynamics auxiliary loss on encoder and predicted latents lifted a multi-small-object pushing benchmark (SLIM) from 0.003 to 0.35 (hard tier 0 -> 0.16); vanilla LeWM failed because its latent was "nearly action-insensitive" and rollouts were no better than copying the current latent; every configuration below an action-sensitivity probe threshold failed to plan — [Strohm et al. 2026, arXiv 2610.03137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03137). Criterion-aligned auxiliary regression of success-relevant quantities: +3.5 pp PushT, +3.4 pp Cube — [Hara & Suzuki 2026, arXiv 2610.01224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01224). Control-Geometry Straightening: up to +20 pp over LeWM for sampling planners at 128 candidates — [Fu & Ning 2026, arXiv 2609.35603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35603)

### Inferences
- Our "more search does nothing with the learned model (24-36%)" matches two separate PushT findings: extra optimization exploits the model (2610.37891) and extra search on a final-goal objective adds nothing (AP Table 9: 2.3% vs 1.6%). The two explanations predict different diagnostics: exploitation shows as predicted-vs-realized cost optimism growing with iterations; a bad target shows as stalls (agent not moving / not walking around) with honest predicted costs. Log both.
- Before anything else, check that our CEM rolls the model out on actions clipped to the env bounds (iCEM clips samples; the LeWM practitioner found unclipped-vs-clipped comparisons inflated optimism ~12x).
- Our cost adds mean_t ||z_t - z_goal||^2 to the terminal term. LeWM, DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC use terminal-only costs, and AP shows that penalizing distance-to-goal along the way rejects detours. A terminal-only ablation is cheap and targets the walk-around failures directly; this is inference, not a tested result for our setup.
- An ensemble of 3-5 MLP predictors on our frozen 32-d encoder is cheap; a variance penalty (PLDM-style, gamma^t weighted) is the standard guard against exploitation, but no PushT number for it was found.
- The PushT predictor-ranking result (Spearman 0.193 for LeWM) suggests even the published LeWM ranks candidates poorly on PushT and wins by executing whole 25-step plans on 25-step goals; it argues for shrinking the distance the model must rank over (near targets) rather than more samples.

### Gaps
- No source reported the PLDM uncertainty-penalty ablation numbers (Appendix E was not in the extracted text) or any ensemble penalty on PushT.
- No PushT ablation of terminal-only vs terminal+running cost was found.
- No PushT ablation of replan cadence (execute first block vs whole plan) was found; only TwoRoom (PLDM).

## Planning with values / learned temporal-distance costs instead of raw latent L2

### Takeaway
Replacing latent L2 with a learned temporal-distance ("steps-to-reach") cost gives the largest reported gains on navigation (TwoRoom 26% -> 98% at offset 100 with nothing else changed), but on contact tasks like PushT the evidence is thinner: Temporal-Distance JEPA uses its mined cost as a training signal rather than as the deployed cost for PushT/Cube, and offline value-based methods (TD-MPC2, IQL/HIQL) score poorly on PushT as planners or policies.

### Cited Findings
- **Learned temporal cost on frozen LeWM (TwoRoom).** A small symmetrised MLP on (z_t, z_goal) trained to predict frame separation within episodes (held-out MAE 12.8 frames; v2 trained on imagined pairs from predictor rollouts: MAE 7.37 real / 8.21 imagined). CEM 300 / 30 elites / 30 iterations, horizon 5 blocks x frameskip 5, unchanged. Results: offset 25 / budget 50: latent L2 94%, decoded-position 92%, temporal head 98%. Offset 100 / budget 150: 26% / 88% / 98%. Offset 100 / budget 50: 20% vs 92% (temporal). Authors' released checkpoint, offset 100: 14% / 70% / 34% (v2 head). The temporal head "learned reachability, not proximity": it charges 24% more to cross the wall, where latent L2 charges 4% less — [Singh 2026, arXiv 2608.12959](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12959). No PushT experiments.
- **Temporal-Distance JEPA.** Mines a directed temporal cost from reward-free trajectories (same-trajectory order as positives, cross-trajectory pairs as negatives) plus a rollout-consistency term matching the planner horizon; used as the deployed cost "when progress is topological" and as a representation signal "when contact geometry dominates". TwoRoom 100.0% vs LeWM 97.4%; Cube +14.2 pp with Euclidean planning on the temporally trained checkpoint; PushT "improved" with no number in the abstract — [Bai & Xiong 2026, arXiv 2607.25337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25337)
- **Value-shaped JEPA latents.** Encoder trained with an IQL value loss so ||E(s) - E(g)|| approximates negative goal-conditioned value; quasimetric variant best. MPPI planning success: Wall-small 0.71 (VF_quasi) vs 0.55 (pred_VCReg, PLDM-style), Wall-big 0.96 vs 0.89, Maze 0.63 vs 0.54; adding a prediction loss to the value loss hurt. Authors argue latent-distance cost "can have numerous local minima". MPPI: Wall 2000 samples, std 12, lambda 0.005, horizon 96 or 64; Maze 500 samples, std 5, lambda 0.0025, horizon 100. No PushT — [Destrade et al. 2025, arXiv 2601.00844](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00844)
- Quasimetric / temporal-distance representation learning for offline GCRL (TMD) and Projective Quasimetric Planning exist but are evaluated as policies/planners outside PushT in the material found — [Myers et al. 2025, arXiv 2509.20478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20478v2); [Kobanda et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.18847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18847v2)
- AP's "rank final" variant (rank retrieved blocks against the final goal) gets 25.8% on long PushT vs 66.4% when ranking against the anchored near target, i.e., the target matters more than the ranking function there — [arXiv 2609.30036](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30036v4)
- **Value-based baselines on PushT.** stable-worldmodel: TD-MPC2 (MPPI/CEM warm-started by the actor, terminal Q with uncertainty penalty) 12% PushT, 4% Cube; IQL 20%, IVL 33%, GCBC 75%; LeWM 96%, PLDM 78%, DINO-WM 74% (50-step budget) — [stable-worldmodel baselines](https://galilai-group.github.io/stable-worldmodel/baselines/). HWM: HIQL 55 / 30 / 20% at d = 25 / 50 / 75 — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- TD-MPC2 planner defaults (short horizon + learned terminal value): horizon 3, 6 iterations, 512 samples, 64 elites, 24 policy trajectories mixed in, min_std 0.05, max_std 2, temperature 0.5 — [tdmpc2 config.yaml](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2/blob/main/tdmpc2/config.yaml)

### Inferences
- A temporal-distance head on our frozen 32-d embeddings is cheap (Singh: small MLP, no retraining of encoder/predictor) and is the most direct test of "is the cost the problem". But our own decomposition (sim + latent cost 86%) says the cost is decent with true dynamics, so a learned cost mainly helps if it also smooths over predictor error; evidence for pushing is indirect. Train it on imagined pairs (Singh v2) so it scores the predictor's own rollouts.
- For walk-around pushes, a temporal-distance cost should in principle rate "go behind the block" states as closer than latent L2 does (reachability vs proximity), which is exactly AP's detour issue; untested on PushT in the sources.
- Offline value-based planning (TD-MPC2-style terminal value) is the weakest option on PushT in the reported baselines (12%); not a priority.

### Gaps
- No PushT number for a learned temporal-distance cost deployed in CEM was found (Temporal-Distance JEPA reports only "improved").
- No reported use of HIQL/QRL value functions as a CEM cost on PushT.

## What DINO-WM, LeWM, PLDM, V-JEPA 2-AC (and TD-MPC2) use; gradient-based and hybrid planning

### Takeaway
All of these use sampling-based MPC (CEM, or MPPI for PLDM/TD-MPC2) with terminal-only latent costs except PLDM; on PushT, gradient-based planning never beats CEM in the sources (LeWM: Adam 84% vs CEM 96%, SGD 26%; DINO-WM: GD 0.28 vs CEM 0.86), and a 2026 PushT study finds Adam 7/16 vs a learned planner 16/16; gradient planning becomes competitive only with model-side changes (train-time data synthesis, straightening).

### Cited Findings
- **LeWM:** CEM, 300 samples, top 30 elites, 30 iterations on PushT (10 on other envs per paper text), init mean 0 / var 1; horizon 5 blocks with frameskip 5 (25 env steps); terminal-only cost; whole plan executed before replanning (App. D); PushT eval budget 50 steps, goal 25 steps ahead — [Maes et al. 2026, arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312)
- LeWM solver ablation on PushT (Table 10), LeWM / PLDM: CEM 96.0 / 78.0; SGD 26 / 4.67; RMSProp 67.33 / 49.33; Adam 84 / 80. PushT: DINO-WM 92.0, PLDM 78.0, LeWM 96.0 — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312)
- **DINO-WM:** cost terminal-only; frameskip 5 and history 3 for PushT; planning table (Table 8) PushT success CEM 0.86 (open loop), GD 0.28 (open loop, all actions executed), MPC with CEM 0.90; PointMaze 0.8 / 0.22 / 0.98; paper gives no analysis of why GD underperforms — [Zhou et al. 2024, arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2). Official CEM config reported by another paper: 300 samples, 30 elites, var scale 1, 30 iterations (search snippet) — [arXiv 2608.24855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24855)
- **PLDM:** MPPI in all planning experiments; replans every step by default (every 4 for Diverse PointMaze); ensemble-uncertainty term; MPPI sample/temperature values not in the extracted text — [Sobal et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14819v4)
- **V-JEPA 2-AC:** CEM, Gaussians init at zero mean / unit variance, 800 samples, 10 iterations, horizon 1, about 16 s per action, terminal L1 energy, actions in an L1 ball of radius 0.075, first action executed then replan — [Assran et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1). A user's V-JEPA 2-AC config: rollout 2, 64 samples, top-k 5, 10 CEM steps, momentum_mean 0.15, momentum_std 0.25, maxnorm 0.075 — [vjepa2 issue #169](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2/issues/169)
- **HWM** (DINO-WM / PLDM / V-JEPA 2-AC backbones): CEM for Franka and PushT, MPPI for maze; Franka high level 3000 samples, 22 elites, 15 iterations, EMA 0.65; low 800 samples, 12 elites, 5 iterations, EMA 0.25 — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208)
- **Adam vs CEM on PushT, 2026:** tuned Adam solved 7/16 held-out scenes, adapted iCEM 2/16, released LePlanner 16/16 — [arXiv 2610.37891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37891)
- **Train-time fixes for gradient planning:** train-time data synthesis lets gradient-based planning match or beat CEM on manipulation and navigation in 10% of CEM's time budget (abstract) — [Agrawal et al. 2025, arXiv 2512.09929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09929). Temporal straightening (curvature regularizer) makes latent Euclidean distance a better proxy for geodesic distance and gradient planning more stable; no numbers in the abstract — [Wang et al. 2026, arXiv 2603.12231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12231)
- **Interleaved CEM + GD (Grad+CEM):** one GD step per CEM iteration on all samples, then refit to the top K; on PlaNet learned models significant gains only on Pendulum (p = 0.019) and Half-Cheetah (p = 0.004), not significant on Cartpole-SwingUp, Walker, Reacher, Cartpole-Balance; pure GD gets stuck in local optima with contact — [Bharadhwaj, Xie, Shkurti 2020, arXiv 2004.08763](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.08763). CEM-GD (CEM init, then GD refinement) — [Huang et al. 2021, arXiv 2112.07746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.07746.pdf)
- A user asked the LeWM authors about GD vs CEM; no answer posted — [le-wm issue #65](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/65)

### Inferences
- With exploitation suspected, gradient planning is the wrong direction: it follows the model's gradient into exactly the regions where it is wrong, and every PushT comparison found favours CEM. Low priority.
- Our CEM is already close to LeWM's (300 samples, 30 elites; we use 10 iterations vs LeWM's 30 on PushT, but more iterations did not help us). The material differences from LeWM are: our running cost term, our replanning every knot (LeWM executes all 25 steps), and our 20-step horizon. None of these is a known fix on its own.

### Gaps
- PLDM's MPPI hyperparameters and DINO-WM's GD step size/iterations were not in the extracted text.
- No PushT result for hybrid CEM+GD with a learned latent model was found.

## CEM/MPPI improvements (iCEM, temperature, smoothing, sample efficiency)

### Takeaway
iCEM's tricks (colored noise, keep/shift elites, clipping, execute best) give 2.7-22x sample efficiency, including 7.2-21.9x on manipulation, but they make the optimizer better at minimizing the model's cost, which does not address exploitation; an adapted iCEM solved only 2/16 PushT scenes in one 2026 comparison.

### Cited Findings
- iCEM components: colored (power-law) noise with exponent beta (successful sequences follow beta ~ 1.5; beta 2-4 work), keep 30% of elites across iterations, shift 30% of last elites to the next env step with a random action appended, population decay N_i = max(N gamma^-i, 2K), clip samples to bounds instead of truncated normal, execute the first action of the best-seen sequence and add the mean to the last iteration; horizon 30. Colored noise has the largest single effect, then keep/shift elites and execute-best; effects are larger at low budget — [Pinneri et al. 2020, arXiv 2008.06389](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.06389v1)
- iCEM manipulation (ground-truth simulator), budget to reach 90% of best baseline: Fetch Pick&Place 1330 -> 185 samples (7.2x), Door 985 -> 45 (21.9x), Relocate 1300 -> 95 (13.7x); overall 2.7-21.9x across 5 tasks — [arXiv 2008.06389](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.06389v1)
- iCEM with learned PlaNet models: budget 366 / 3 iterations "not far behind" CEM with 10,000 / 10 iterations; 0.044 s vs 0.18 s per step — [arXiv 2008.06389](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.06389v1)
- Adapted iCEM on 16 held-out PushT scenes with a learned latent model: 2/16 solved, 49 s total — [arXiv 2610.37891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37891)
- MPPI temperatures in practice: TD-MPC2 temperature 0.5 with 64 elites of 512 samples, std clamped to [0.05, 2] — [tdmpc2 config](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2/blob/main/tdmpc2/config.yaml); Destrade et al. MPPI lambda 0.005 (2000 samples) and 0.0025 (500 samples) — [arXiv 2601.00844](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00844)
- Momentum/EMA smoothing of the CEM distribution: HWM PushT EMA 0.8-0.9 (low/high level), Franka 0.25 / 0.65 — [arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03208); V-JEPA 2-AC user config momentum_mean 0.15, momentum_std 0.25 — [vjepa2 issue #169](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2/issues/169)
- LeWM public checkpoints, CEM samples/iterations/elites: Reacher 300/10/30 -> 72%, 300/30/30 -> 80%; Cube 78% vs 76%; PushT 300/30/30 -> 98% (single user run) — [le-wm issue #41](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/41)
- Control-Geometry Straightening reports finite-budget guarantees for MPPI and local contraction for CEM under linear dynamics, and its gains are measured at 128 candidates per update — [arXiv 2609.35603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35603)

### Inferences
- iCEM's keep/shift-elites is close to our warm start by shifting; colored noise is similar in spirit to our knot interpolation (both make actions temporally smooth). Expect little change in success; iCEM is a speed tool (could let us cut 300x10 to ~100x3 and spend compute on ensembles or anchors).
- If we keep CEM, a higher momentum (HWM uses 0.8-0.9 on PushT; ours is 0.1) or a std floor keeps the distribution from collapsing onto a narrow exploit; untested on our setup.

### Gaps
- iCEM's supplementary beta/gamma values per task were not in the extracted text.
- No PushT-specific MPPI vs CEM comparison with a learned latent model was found.

## Practitioner experience: GitHub issues, reproductions, evaluation pitfalls

### Takeaway
The public issue trackers contain no thread where a PushT planning failure was fixed by a planner change; the practical lessons are about evaluation (50-episode noise of +-5-6 pp, evaluation on training episodes, infinite vs 50-step budgets, config mismatches between paper and code) and about predictor training details (supervise every context position because the planner starts from one frame).

### Cited Findings
- LeWM PushT, same checkpoint, CEM, 50 episodes, 5 seeds: 88 / 84 / 96 / 92 / 80% (+-5-6 pp noise floor); a commenter attributes most of it to start/goal sampling — [le-wm issue #82](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/82)
- LeWM eval configs sample start/goal from the training dataset (`pusht_expert_train`), goal 25 steps later in the same episode; the authors confirmed evaluation on randomly selected training clips (issue #21); DINO-WM plans on a trajectory-level validation split — [le-wm issue #111](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/111)
- TwoRoom reproduction failed (20-40%) with paper budget/offset 150/100; v3 of the paper corrects TwoRoom to 50/25, which reproduces — [le-wm issue #72](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/72). One user reports 88% TwoRoom but only 60% PushT when training from scratch on A6000 (unresolved) — [le-wm issue #72](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/72)
- Paper says 10 CEM iterations outside PushT, released config uses 30 everywhere — [le-wm issue #41](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/41)
- Retrained predictor with 11x lower latent error planned 36% vs 85% on TwoRoom because only the last of 3 context positions was supervised while the planner starts from 1 frame; with the official loss it planned 84-89% — [le-wm issue #107](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/107)
- Released PushT planning YAML uses history size 1 even though the harness allows 3; published controller executes all 25 planned commands before replanning, so changing only population size does not reproduce it (search snippet, attribution to this paper unverified) — [arXiv 2610.37891 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37891)
- DINO-WM's `planner.max_iter: null` means replanning continues until success (effectively infinite budget); stable-worldmodel re-evaluates with a fixed 50-step budget and gets DINO-WM PushT 74% (vs 90% in the paper) — [dino_wm issue #29](https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm/issues/29); [stable-worldmodel baselines](https://galilai-group.github.io/stable-worldmodel/baselines/)
- DINO-WM's MPC loop uses environment success (`env.eval_state`) to mask further actions and stop planning for successful trajectories, so success is "reached at any point" — [dino_wm issue #26](https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm/issues/26)
- DINO-WM rope: imagined rollouts reach the goal while the real rope does not (classic model exploitation / drift); unresolved, second user cannot reproduce rope or granular — [dino_wm issue #24](https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm/issues/24)
- V-JEPA 2-AC in Isaac Lab: arm consistently stops a few cm above the cube and never grasps, with both "cube moved" and "cube held" goal images; no fix posted — [vjepa2 issue #169](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2/issues/169). The V-JEPA 2-AC paper itself needed three subgoal images for pick-and-place — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- A ridge bilinear predictor on LeWM's frozen PushT encoder planned 35-40% vs 85-94% for neural predictors (preregistered, 4 seeds x 100 episodes), while it beat them on TwoRoom — [sahnas summary](https://github.com/sahnas/stigmergic-module-routing/blob/main/docs/summary_two_pages.md)
- Community ports (Rust/Burn LeWM PushT checkpoint, PyTorch reimplementation) list targets but no verified CEM reproduction — [lewm-rs-pusht](https://huggingface.co/abdelstark/lewm-rs-pusht); [lewm-implementation](https://huggingface.co/ar27111994/lewm-implementation)
- An issue titled "Add hierarchical planning modules" (inverse dynamics, conditioned predictor, anchored AR modules for hierarchical LeWM) was closed with no body or discussion — [le-wm issue #40](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm/issues/40)

### Inferences
- With 50 episodes, our 32% vs 39% gap is inside the observed noise; use 200+ episodes or paired seeds (McNemar on paired episodes, as the sahnas reproduction did) before trusting deltas under ~10 pp.
- Published PushT numbers (LeWM 96%, DINO-WM 90%) are on 25-step goals, in some cases from training episodes and with generous budgets; our 20-step-horizon tuning tasks with walk-arounds are harder than the benchmark protocol, so 32-39% is not directly comparable.
- If our MLP predictor uses a history/chunk context, check its error at the shortest context the planner actually uses at episode start (issue #107 lesson).

### Gaps
- I found no blog post walking through a PushT planning failure and its fix; practitioner material is in GitHub issues and one Kaggle reproduction repo.
- No responses from LeWM or DINO-WM authors on planner-side fixes (issues #65 and #107 Q3 on SIGReg and exploitation went unanswered by authors).
- A search snippet reported that the start/goal "query sampling rule" moves PushT success from 60.9% to 82.8% and that two implementations agree on 599/600 episodes, but I could not attribute it to a specific paper, so it is not cited above.
